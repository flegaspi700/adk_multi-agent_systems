Logline: In a world of corsets and calculations, a visionary mathematician defies societal constraints to unlock the true potential of machines, becoming the world's first computer programmer and forever changing the course of human innovation.

Synopsis:
Act I: The Burden of Genius
Young Ada Byron, brilliant and inquisitive, chafes under the strictures of Victorian society and her mother's attempts to suppress her "poetic" imagination, fearing she will follow in her infamous father, Lord Byron's, footsteps. Despite her mother's efforts to ground her in logic and mathematics, Ada finds herself drawn to the fantastical possibilities within numbers. Her world changes when she meets the eccentric inventor Charles Babbage and is introduced to his Analytical Engine, a mechanical marvel that sparks her imagination beyond mere arithmetic. She sees a future for the machine that Babbage himself hasn't fully grasped, igniting a fierce ambition within her to understand and expand its capabilities.

Act II: The Algorithm and the Vision
Ada dedicates herself to Babbage's Analytical Engine, immersing herself in its complex mechanics and theoretical applications. She faces skepticism from male contemporaries and the constant pressure to fulfill her societal role as a wife and mother, often battling ill health. Despite these obstacles, she perseveres, developing the world's first algorithm intended for the machine – a groundbreaking step that envisions computers not just as calculators, but as tools for creation, capable of composing music and generating art. She struggles to get her revolutionary ideas recognized and published, eventually doing so under initials to circumvent gender bias, all while grappling with personal sacrifices and the isolation of her unique intellectual pursuit.

Act III: Legacy and the Future Foreseen
As Ada's health declines, her resolve to complete and publish her comprehensive notes on the Analytical Engine intensifies. She faces a final push to articulate her profound vision for the machine, battling against those who dismiss her work as mere translation or a woman's fanciful musings. Her published notes, including the seminal algorithm, lay the theoretical groundwork for the future of computing, even if the full scope of her foresight is not immediately understood by her contemporaries. The film concludes with Ada's passing, but her legacy endures, hinting at the future generations who will build upon her foundational ideas, fulfilling her prophecy of a world transformed by intelligent machines, a testament to her enduring genius and pioneering spirit.
